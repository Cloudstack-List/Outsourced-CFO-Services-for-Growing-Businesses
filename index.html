In Short

Outsourced CFO services give a business senior financial leadership on a part-time basis, usually a few days a month. The same function is also sold as a fractional CFO or a virtual CFO, since the three terms overlap heavily. A virtual CFO simply means the work is delivered remotely.

The work covers cash forecasting, financial modeling, unit economics, pricing analysis, technical accounting, and board or lender reporting. A bookkeeper records what happened. Then a controller confirms it was recorded correctly and closes the books on time. Finally, the CFO decides what to do about it.

Same Job, Different Labels

Fractional CFO, Virtual CFO, or Outsourced CFO?

Buyers search all three terms, although providers use them interchangeably. For instance, a virtual CFO listing and an outsourced CFO listing often describe identical work. The label matters less than what lands on your desk, although the differences are worth knowing before you sign.

TermWhat It Usually MeansWhere It Falls Short
Fractional CFOTypically an individual working part-time across several companies, usually a set number of days per monthA fractional CFO is one person. Technical accounting outside their experience goes unhandled, and holidays leave a gap
Virtual CFOThe same function delivered remotely. In effect a virtual CFO arrangement describes how the work happens, rather than who does itThe virtual CFO label says nothing about seniority or depth, since a bookkeeper working remotely can use it too
Outsourced CFOA firm delivering the function, with a lead advisor plus specialists behind themCosts more than a solo fractional CFO on paper. Nevertheless the bench usually removes the need for a second provider

How to Choose

If your accounting is straightforward and you mainly need forecasting and reporting discipline, a fractional CFO or a virtual CFO usually covers it, and it costs less. Either arrangement works. If you hit ASC 606, ASC 718, convertible instruments, multi-state nexus, or IRC 280E, a solo fractional CFO or virtual CFO will either refer the work out or guess at it. That is where an outsourced CFO firm earns the difference, because the technical work stays in house.

In practice GreenGrowth CPAs is structured as the third option. You get a named lead advisor for the fractional CFO relationship, working as a virtual CFO or on site as you prefer, with tax, audit, and technical accounting specialists behind them.

01

Usually First

Cash Flow and Runway Management

A profit and loss statement tells you whether the period was profitable. Nevertheless, it cannot tell you whether payroll clears in six weeks. Those are different questions, and profitable companies fail on the second one regularly.

  • Rolling thirteen-week forecast tracking actual cash in and out, updated weekly
  • Working capital planning across receivables, payables and inventory
  • Runway analysis tied directly to hiring and capital decisions
  • Collections and payment timing worked as levers rather than accepted as given

This is almost always the first build, because it answers the question that keeps founders awake. See our guide to financial dashboards for how the thirteen-week view gets reported.

02

Planning

Budgeting, Forecasting and FP&A

Most growing companies budget once a year and then discover in month four that the plan no longer describes the business. A working forecast updates against actuals and tells you what changed.

  • Annual budget built bottom up from drivers rather than last year plus a percentage
  • Rolling forecast reforecast monthly against actual performance
  • Variance analysis explaining why the gap exists rather than only that it does
  • Headcount and capacity planning tied to the revenue it supports
  • Departmental budgets owners can actually be held to
03

Every Month

Monthly Reporting and Financial Close

If your financials arrive six weeks after month end, they are history rather than information. In practice a realistic target is day ten, and getting there is a process problem rather than an effort problem.

  • Close calendar with owners and deadlines for every step
  • Reconciliations standardized, so that they stop being rebuilt each month
  • Accrual and cut-off procedures applied consistently period to period
  • Chart of accounts designed around the decisions you actually make
  • Systems integrated across point of sale, payroll, billing and the ledger

Most reporting problems are structural. Where the chart of accounts was never designed for how the business reports, no amount of analysis fixes the output.

04

Where Margin Hides

KPIs, Unit Economics and Profitability

Most companies know their overall margin and very little beneath it. Consequently they cannot say which product, location or customer segment actually earns its keep once real costs are allocated.

  • Margin broken out by product, location, service line or customer cohort
  • Cost allocation that attaches overhead where it genuinely belongs
  • Contribution margin and breakeven by segment rather than in aggregate
  • KPI dashboard reporting the handful of numbers that actually move decisions
  • Pricing analysis testing whether current pricing still holds

Where nobody can answer a margin question without a week of manual work, the data structure needs attention before the strategy does.

05

Before You Commit

Financial Modeling and Scenario Planning

Every significant decision is a bet on a set of assumptions. A model makes those assumptions visible, so the conversation moves from instinct to arithmetic.

  • Integrated three-statement model linking profit, balance sheet and cash
  • Scenario planning showing what happens if growth halves or doubles
  • Hiring, expansion and capital expenditure decisions modeled before commitment
  • Debt versus equity analysis, including covenant headroom under each path
  • Sensitivity testing on the two or three assumptions that actually matter
06

Outside Scrutiny

Board, Investor and Lender Reporting

Reporting to outsiders is a different discipline from reporting internally. The numbers have to survive questions from people who did not build them and who compare you against other companies.

  • Board packages structured around decisions rather than around data
  • Investor updates with consistent metrics reported the same way every period
  • Covenant compliance tracking and early warning before a breach happens
  • Lender reporting packages built to the format the agreement specifies
  • Diligence-ready records, so an investor request does not stop operations

07

Where Most Providers Stop

Technical Accounting and Transaction Support

This layer requires technical accounting expertise beyond routine bookkeeping and monthly reporting, and it is where an engagement either holds up under an audit or does not. Each area below carries its own conclusion that has to be documented.

  • Revenue recognition under ASC 606. Contract review against the five-step model, performance obligations, transaction price allocation, and a written revenue policy most private companies never produced
  • Stock-based compensation under ASC 718. Grant date determination, fair value measurement, expense recognition across vesting, and modification accounting
  • Equity and liability instruments. Convertible notes, SAFEs, warrants and preferred rounds, each carrying a classification question plus embedded derivative analysis
  • Inventory costing. Methodology, valuation and reserves, cost of goods sold tracking, and shrinkage or dead stock analysis
  • Business combination accounting. Purchase price allocation, opening balance sheet and post-close integration

Outsourced CFO FAQs

The Basics

What are outsourced CFO services?

Outsourced CFO services give a business senior financial leadership on a part-time basis, usually a few days a month. The work covers cash forecasting, financial modeling, unit economics, pricing analysis, technical accounting, and board or lender reporting. It is the strategic layer above bookkeeping and controllership, delivered without a full-time executive salary.

What is the difference between a bookkeeper, a controller, and a CFO?

A bookkeeper records what happened, handling transactions, invoicing, and reconciliation. Meanwhile a controller confirms it was recorded correctly, owning the monthly close, accuracy, and internal controls. Finally, the CFO decides what to do about it, covering cash forecasting, pricing, growth options, and outside reporting. All three matter, and companies commonly hire the first when the gap is really the third.

Is a fractional CFO the same as an outsourced CFO?

In practice the terms overlap and often get used interchangeably. Fractional CFO usually describes an individual working part-time across several companies. Outsourced CFO describes a firm delivering the function, which means bench depth and continuity if one person is unavailable. Ask what the engagement actually delivers rather than which label a provider uses.

What is the difference between a virtual CFO and a fractional CFO?

Virtual CFO describes how the work is delivered, meaning remotely rather than on site. Fractional CFO describes how much you buy, meaning part of a person's time. The two labels answer different questions, so they are not alternatives. A fractional CFO can work virtually, and a virtual CFO can be full time. Neither label tells you anything about seniority or technical depth. Ask about both directly.

Timing and Technical Work

When should a business hire an outsourced CFO rather than a full-time one?

Revenue is a poor trigger on its own. Instead, the stronger signals are a founder running finance personally, or a monthly close taking six weeks. Others include an inability to answer a margin question quickly, an investor about to review the numbers, or a decision postponed for a quarter. A full-time CFO usually makes sense at greater scale or when preparing for a listing or a large acquisition.

Can GreenGrowth CPAs handle technical accounting like ASC 606 and ASC 718?

Yes. Specifically, our team handles revenue recognition under ASC 606 and stock-based compensation under ASC 718. We also cover equity and liability instrument classification, including convertible notes and warrants, plus inventory costing and business combination accounting. These areas require technical accounting expertise beyond routine bookkeeping and monthly financial reporting.

Can the same firm do our CFO work and our audit?

Generally not, where the audit supports an SEC filing. Independence rules prevent a firm auditing statements it helped produce, and outsourced CFO work falls squarely in that category. Companies planning a listing usually split the relationship well before filing, which is a reason to raise the question early rather than during diligence.
